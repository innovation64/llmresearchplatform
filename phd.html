PhD Research Agenda

The through-line of my doctoral work is simple to state and hard to do: memory is the substrate of self-improvement. An agent that cannot durably remember what worked cannot reliably get better. The directions below run from what exists today toward what is still exploratory; the later ones are intentionally described at the level of direction rather than method.

Recursive Self-Improvement & Self-Evolving Agents

Exploratory

Studying how an agent can close the loop between experience and capability: accumulating durable experience, evaluating its own trajectories, revising its own strategies, prompts, and tooling, and compounding those revisions over time. The open questions I care about are what makes such a loop converge rather than drift, how improvement is measured when the evaluator is also changing, and what verification envelope keeps self-modification bounded and auditable. Directional, early-stage work — specifics deliberately not detailed here.

Brain-Inspired Memory Architectures for LLMs

Building on the BMAM framework to develop neuroscience-principled memory systems that enable agents with human-like consolidation, forgetting, and retrieval capabilities.

Multi-Agent Evaluation Frameworks

Developing robust methodologies to evaluate collaboration, specialization, and information sharing between multiple LLM-based agents in complex task environments.

Context-Aware Reasoning for Ambiguous Tasks

Building on CondAmbigQA research (EMNLP 2025) to enable LLMs to handle ambiguity through contextual understanding and multi-step reasoning processes.

Domain-Specific LLMs for Healthcare

Extending Pain LLM research to broader healthcare applications, leveraging experience with distributed training on large GPU clusters and behavioral benchmarking.

RAG Architecture for Specialized Domains

Exploring retrieval-augmented generation techniques optimized for domain-specific knowledge integration, focusing on medical and scientific applications.

AURA: Intent-Directed Probing for Implicit-Need Surfacing in Situated LLM Agents

Y. Li, J. Liu, J. Cai, and M. Xu. arXiv:2606.05557

Adds an inference layer between scene perception and tool execution: an IntentFrame assesses the unstated needs behind a situated query, and a gap score decides how much probing each query deserves — broader implicit-need coverage than ReAct-style probing, with 82% fewer probes on factual lookups.
